I'll say it plainly: to me, the Auburn 851 Speedster is the most beautiful car ever built. The long louvred hood with
those four chrome pipes coming out of its side, the V windscreen, the fenders that flow back into the running boards,
and that boattail ending in a point. Gordon Buehrig drew it in 1935 with almost no money behind him, and Auburn
still put a plaque on every dashboard signed by Ab Jenkins, saying the car had done over 100 mph before it was
delivered.

So this one didn't get the usual treatment. It got the special one.

![The Auburn 851 Speedster from behind, boattail and spear](/images/assetto-corsa/auburn-851-sc-speedster-1935/shot-rear-three-quarter.jpg "The boattail")

I started from Kryox Shade's lovely model on Sketchfab and reworked it piece by piece, with photographs of the real
survivors open beside it the whole time: the V grille with its 851 figures, the Flying Goddess on the hood, the
windscreen and its chrome frame, the spot lamps, the dipped bumpers, the boattail and its spear, the dash with its two
pods of gauges, the pleated leather. The doors open, the wipers sweep, the centre mirror works, the spot lamps light
up with the headlamps. There is a whole underside too, X-braced frame, axles, springs and exhaust, because it would
have been a shame to roll it in a race and see nothing.

![The Flying Goddess and the V grille](/images/assetto-corsa/auburn-851-sc-speedster-1935/shot-mascot.jpg "The Flying Goddess")

![The red car from the side](/images/assetto-corsa/auburn-851-sc-speedster-1935/shot-side.jpg "The side view")

It drives like a big 1935 car should: a supercharged Lycoming straight-eight with 150 bhp, its own engine sound made in
engine-sim, a three-speed box, drum brakes, beam axles and an ahooga horn.

## The Dual-Ratio axle

The thing I'm proudest of is the Columbia Dual-Ratio axle, working as it did. The real car had a lever on the
steering-wheel hub that preselects a low or a high axle ratio, and the axle shifts the next time you press the clutch.
In the game:

- The car starts in **high**. Drive it like any three-speed.
- Press **Extra A**: low is preselected, and you'll see the lever on the hub swing over.
- Press the clutch (or change gear) **below 40 mph** and the axle shifts. The gear display shows 1L, 2L, 3L or 1H, 2H, 3H.
- Press Extra A again, then the clutch, to go back to high.

**About Extra A**, because I know some of you have had trouble with the extra keys: it's a Custom Shaders Patch key,
and its default is Alt+Numpad 7. No numpad? Go to Content Manager → Settings → Assetto Corsa → Controls → Patch tab,
group "Car (extras)", click **Extra A** and press any key or wheel button you like, then save. It's a toggle, one press
each way. And if it still won't play along, don't worry: the car just stays in high, and it drives beautifully there.
