Some cars were built to get you there. This one was built to make sure everybody saw you arrive.

In January 1930, a few months after the Crash, Cadillac unveiled a car nobody else in the world could match: a
sixteen-cylinder engine, 452 cubic inches, smooth and almost silent at idle. Even the engine was styled: the
wiring and plumbing hidden away, the rest polished or enamelled, because the man who bought a Sixteen was going
to lift the hood and show it to his friends. Fleetwood built the bodies by hand,
and the two-passenger roadster, style 4302, was the "cheap" one: $5,350, at a time when a new Ford cost under $500.
They made 105 of them.

![The V-16 Roadster out in the yard](/images/assetto-corsa/cadillac-v16-roadster-1930/shot-front.jpg "The V-16 Roadster")

Ours is built on Kryox Shade's gorgeous model, the same artist behind our Auburn Speedster, and we spent a long time
with photographs of a surviving car before we called it done. Look at the front: the woven grille with its
shutters, the fluted headlamp glass, the V-16 badge standing on its rod, the Cadillac crest, the twin Pilot-Ray
lamps on the screen pillars. Look at the wheels: chrome V16 hub caps, two spares sitting in the front fenders,
whitewalls all round. And behind the seat, the top lies folded in its tan canvas boot, the way you'd drive it on a
summer afternoon.

![Trunk on the rack, side-mounts in the fenders](/images/assetto-corsa/cadillac-v16-roadster-1930/shot-rear.jpg "From behind")

Then open the door, because the inside is where this car really shows off. The dash is engine-turned aluminium
with a chrome-rimmed panel and six period dials, and every one of them works: oil, fuel, the eight-day clock, the
120 mph speedometer, the ammeter and the temperature gauge. The bench is pleated leather, built again from scratch
until it looked like the real one. The steering wheel is the big black four-spoke of the period, and there's a mirror
hanging from the middle of the windscreen frame, just like the photo car's.

![Lamps lit: headlamps, Pilot-Rays and parking lamps](/images/assetto-corsa/cadillac-v16-roadster-1930/shot-night.jpg "Lamps lit")

Driving it is an occasion. 175 horsepower is not much by today's standards, but this engine gives you over 300
lb-ft from just above idle, so the car simply rolls away in any gear with a deep, smooth hum, and it was louder than
we first gave it credit for, so we turned it up. It weighs two and a half tonnes and stops on 1930 mechanical drums:
plan your braking well ahead, sit back, and enjoy the scenery from the best seat on the road.

![Red and black, the model's own scheme](/images/assetto-corsa/cadillac-v16-roadster-1930/shot-side.jpg "Red and black")

It comes with everything our cars carry now: doors that open, vacuum wipers, wheels that blur at speed, an axle and
springs that move with the suspension, a full chassis underneath with the V16 and its gearbox, Kunos-style damage
and dirt (set Damage above 0 % in the assists), and a driver and pit crew dressed for each skin. Eight skins, most of
them real cars from auction records: Longfellow Green, Boone Brown with Gold Bronze wheels, black with Tokio Ivory,
two-tone grey and blue, two-tone red, dark grey, the sage green car with chrome wheels we used as our reference, and
the model's own red and black.
