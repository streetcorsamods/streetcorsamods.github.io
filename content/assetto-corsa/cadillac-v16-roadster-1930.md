The 1930 Cadillac V-16, Series 452, with the Fleetwood style 4302 two-passenger roadster body and rumble seat: 105
built, the least expensive Sixteen at $5,350. The 452 cu in 45-degree overhead-valve V16 with hydraulic valve
silencers: 175 bhp at 3400 rpm and 320 lb-ft at 1400, over 300 from idle. Three-speed Synchro-Mesh gearbox, 4.39
axle (3.47, 4.07 and 4.75 in the setup), twin-disc clutch, vacuum-assisted mechanical 16.5 in drums, beam axles on
semi-elliptic springs, 7.50-19 whitewalls on wire wheels, 148 in wheelbase, 5325 lb.

It was reworked to photographs of a surviving car: the engine-turned dash with six working period dials (oil,
fuel, clock, speedometer, ammeter, temperature), the four-spoke wheel and column, a pleated leather bench, kick
panels, carpet and pedals; doors that open, vacuum wipers, a centre mirror on the screen header and chrome side
mirrors; the woven grille and shutters, fluted headlamp glass, the V-16 badge and crest, and Pilot-Ray spot lamps
that light the road; wire wheels with V16 hub caps and twin side-mount spares; the top folded in its boot behind the
seat.

Like all our cars: a full underside with the frame, the V16 and gearbox, and an axle, springs and front beam that
move with the suspension; Kunos-style damage and dirt (set Damage above 0 % in the assists), glass and lamps that
crack, a loose hood after a hard hit, rim blur, six onboard cameras, and a driver and pit crew dressed to match each
skin. Custom Shaders Patch recommended; the car drives without it.

Gearbox ratios, weight distribution and the centre of gravity are estimates: we found no factory data for them. It
is a 2.5-tonne car on 1930 drums: brake early.
