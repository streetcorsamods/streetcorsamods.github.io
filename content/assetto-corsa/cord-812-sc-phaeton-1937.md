Cord 812 Supercharged Phaeton, 1937: Gordon Buehrig's "coffin nose", the front-wheel-drive Cord that stopped the
1935 New York show. The Lycoming FC is a 288.6 cu in L-head V8, and the SC adds a Schwitzer-Cummins centrifugal
supercharger: 170 bhp at 3,500 rpm and 260 lb-ft at 2,200, with the whine to go with it. The four-speed gearbox
sits ahead of the engine and is worked by the Bendix Electric Hand pre-selector on the steering column. Independent
front suspension on trailing arms, a tubular rear axle on leaf springs, Lockheed hydraulic drums, 6.50-16 whitewalls
on painted wheels with holed chrome trim rings, a 125 in wheelbase and about 3,950 lb.

It was the first American car with disappearing headlamps, and so is ours: switch the lights on and they rise out of
the fenders. The engine-turned dash was rebuilt from photographs, with a working speedometer, tachometer, fuel and
water gauges; with Custom Shaders Patch the odometers, clock, oil pressure and ammeter work too. The hood, cowl,
trunk lid, top-well lid and fuel filler are real separate panels, and with CSP the trunk, cowl vents and filler door
open on Extra A, B and C. Rear-hinged doors that open, cowl wipers, chrome flex pipes, wrap-round louvres, and the
front-drive running gear, rear axle and springs moving with the wheels underneath.

Like all our cars: four LODs, Kunos-style damage and dirt (set Damage above 0 % in the assists), and a driver and
crew. Custom Shaders Patch recommended; the car drives without it.
