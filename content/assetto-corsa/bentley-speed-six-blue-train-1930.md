Bentley Speed Six, chassis HM2855, with the low fastback Sportsman Coupé body by Gurney Nutting and a sideways rear
seat. In March 1930 Woolf Barnato bet he could drive from Cannes to his London club before Le Train Bleu reached
Calais, and won; the coupé became the famous Blue Train Bentley, though its records show it was finished in May 1930
and the race itself was run in his Mulliner saloon. The 6.6 litre overhead-cam six with four valves a cylinder and
twin SUs makes 180 bhp at 3,500 rpm. C-type four-speed gearbox (3.364/1.823/1.357/1.00) with right-hand change on a
3.53 axle, beam axle and live axle on semi-elliptic leaves, 16 in drums with a Dewandre servo, 21 in Rudge-Whitworth
wire wheels, about 2,150 kg. Right-hand drive.

The dials were redrawn from photographs of the dash: black faces, red-tipped needles, seven of them with the clock,
water and petrol, lit at night. Its own engine sound, and a motor Klaxon for the horn. A period English mirror hangs
from the header on a swan neck, two more sit on the screen pillars, and the head, pass and tail lamps all light.

Like all our cars: rear-hinged doors that open, wipers, a beam axle and rear axle that move with the suspension, rim
blur on the wire wheels, Kunos-style damage and dirt (set Damage above 0 % in the assists), four LODs and a driver and
crew dressed for each skin. Custom Shaders Patch recommended; the car drives without it.

The wheelbase is the 3D model's 3.335 m, shorter than the real car's, and the physics follow it. Weight distribution
and the centre of gravity are estimates.
